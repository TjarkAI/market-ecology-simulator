What is this?

An equity market made of several hundred agents. Each follows a simple rule, none knows the true value, none knows what the others are about to do — and nowhere in the code is there a formula for the price. Every price move is one order meeting another.

The town is the display

Block height
Capital standing in that district. After many trading days one camp holds almost everything and the other districts sit flat — then SCALE · COMPARE at the bottom right makes them distinguishable again (logarithmic, so no longer a height in euros)
Road width
Order flow on that road, in this step
One figure
ten agents carrying an order to the exchange; the crate is green for a buy and red for a sell
Trucks
the ETF depot's scheduled purchases and the issuer's new shares
Five towers
the five exchanges, one per sector. Height = book depth
Numbers above
trades that have just happened
Red ring
margin call: a fund has to sell
Light
sentiment: warm sun when greedy, overcast when fearful, rain in panic
Market panel
left: price against fair value, daily candles, order book — switchable, always the selected market

The districts

Adaptive Quarter
300 traders who regularly switch between trend following and value, depending on what worked last
Noise Suburb
trades without an opinion, supplies the other side
ETF Depot
buys on schedule, regardless of price, reads no news
Leverage Tower
funds on credit. If the price falls too far they must sell — without deciding to
Market Makers
the only ones who quote prices all the time
Issuer
issues new shares when the price stands far above fair value
Central Bank
sets the rate; it acts through valuation, credit cost and flows, never directly on the price

What to try first

  1. Press 16× and watch the price drift away from fair value — the market panel on the left shows both. Candles shows the same move as trading days, Book the resting orders every single price comes from.
  2. Load a scenario: "Bubble formation" or "Leverage crash". It sets conditions and supplies headlines; what the price makes of them is the model's answer, not a script.
  3. Start an experiment. It sets the market up, says what to watch — and then checks, by measurement, whether it worked. Also when it did not.
  4. Click a district, on the map or in the list: the left side says what it is for, the right shows a real member and its decision this step.
  5. Drag the map to turn it, scroll to zoom, right-drag to move it.
  6. Go to Analysis & validation for the sector comparison: correlations, stylized facts, A/B comparison across several seeds.
